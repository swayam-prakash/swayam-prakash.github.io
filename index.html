About

I am an immunologist and vaccine scientist. My research asks a practical question: why do some people control a virus without becoming ill, and can we build that response on purpose? At UC Irvine I helped identify conserved B-cell, CD4 and CD8 T-cell targets shared across human and animal coronaviruses, and advanced multi-epitope and multi-antigen pan-coronavirus vaccine candidates on mRNA-LNP, adenoviral, AAV and peptide platforms.

In herpes simplex infection I study tissue-resident memory T cells, mucosal chemokines and T-cell exhaustion, and the prime-pull-keep strategy for therapeutic vaccination. This work rests on a decade of human immunogenetics (KIR, HLA and transplantation), on single-cell and bulk transcriptomics and immunoinformatics, and on preclinical models from mice to HLA-transgenic rabbits.

I also bring hands-on histocompatibility experience in renal transplantation (HLA typing, antibody screening and crossmatching), and public-health research that spans field studies of chronic kidney disease of unknown etiology in India and 20 papers with the Global Burden of Disease Study.

In 2026 I completed graduate training in artificial intelligence and data science at UC Berkeley and Woolf (Udacity Institute of AI and Technology), with executive education at Harvard Business School. I use it to build interpretable models of protective immunity and to bring AI into vaccine design.

Expertise

  • Vaccinology: multi-epitope and multi-antigen vaccine design on mRNA-LNP, adenoviral, AAV and peptide platforms; preclinical efficacy under BSL-3 and ABSL-3 containment.
  • Cellular immunology: antiviral B-cell, CD4 and CD8 T-cell responses, tissue-resident memory, T-cell exhaustion, flow cytometry, ELISpot and CyTOF.
  • Histocompatibility: molecular and serological HLA typing, crossmatch, PRA and donor-specific antibody testing, KIR genotyping and HLA ligand matching.
  • Computational immunology: single-cell and bulk transcriptomics, immunoinformatics and epitope prediction, genome-wide association analysis.
  • Artificial intelligence and data science: machine learning, interpretable and batch-aware modeling, data science pipelines, and AI in vaccine design and preclinical testing.
